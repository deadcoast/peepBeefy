Design origin:          DOOM PSX fire algorithm, adapted for ASCII character-scale rendering


======================================================================
1. PALETTE GRADIENT (12-step)
======================================================================

Derived from the DOOM PSX fire convention: a continuous gradient from
barely-visible ember through 5 flame colors to a near-white hot tip.
User-specified flame colors sit at even indices (2, 4, 6, 8, 10).
The 7 intermediates exist solely so flicker can reach smoothly between
adjacent flame zones.

Index  Hex       Name               Role
-----  -------   -----------------  ---------------------------------
 00    #4F0F07   dark ember         below Line 5, barely visible
 01    #6D1504   mid ember          transition
 02    #8B1A00   deep ember red     LINE 5 BASE (ember)
 03    #AB1E00   warm ember         transition
 04    #CC2200   dark red-orange    LINE 4 BASE (lower flame)
 05    #E53400   red-orange         transition
 06    #FF4500   orange-red         LINE 3 BASE (flame body)
 07    #FF6800   bright orange      transition
 08    #FF8C00   dark orange        LINE 2 BASE (upper flame)
 09    #FFB400   amber              transition
 10    #FFD700   bright yellow      LINE 1 BASE (flame tips)
 11    #FFE55C   hot yellow         above Line 1, brightest peak

Why 12 and not 8 or 37: The DOOM palette uses 37 colors but targets
pixel-scale resolution. We color individual ASCII characters — 12 steps
is the right density for +/-2-step flicker across 5 flame zones. More
would be wasted (human eye can't distinguish adjacent hex values at
character scale). Fewer would produce visible banding.

install.py constant:  FIRE_PALETTE  (tuple of 12 hex strings)
                      FIRE_STYLES   (tuple of 12 pre-built Style objects)


======================================================================
2. LINE-TO-PALETTE MAPPING
======================================================================

Each flame line (1-5) has a base palette index and a flicker range —
the indices it can reach via random per-character variation.

Line   Zone      Base Index   Flicker Range   Clamp
-----  --------  ----------   -------------   ------
 01    TIPS         10          [8, 11]        max 11
 02    UPPER         8          [6, 10]
 03    BODY          6          [4, 8]
 04    LOWER         4          [2, 6]
 05    EMBER         2          [0, 4]         min 0

Key property: adjacent lines overlap by 2 indices.
Line 1 at -2 = index 8, which is Line 2's base.
Line 2 at +2 = index 10, which is Line 1's base.
This overlap creates the seamless heat diffusion that defines the
DOOM fire look — no hard color boundaries between flame zones.

Text lines (6-8) have a base color/style plus an EMBER CRACK effect:

Line   Zone      Base Color  Style   Crack%  Crack Palette
-----  --------  ----------  ------  ------  ----------------------------
 06    TEXT-HOT  #FFFFFF     bold      20%   #FFD700 (yellow), #FFE55C (hot-yellow)
 07    TEXT-MID  #CCCCCC     normal    12%   #FF8C00 (orange), #FFB400 (amber)
 08    TEXT-DIM  #999999     dim        6%   #8B1A00 (ember),  #CC2200 (dark-red)

Ember cracks: Each frame, every printable character in the text lines rolls
against the crack probability. On success, the character is rendered in one
of the crack palette colors (randomly chosen) instead of its base style.
Space characters are never cracked (same rule as flame flicker).

The effect simulates embers glowing through cracks in cooling stone.
Crack positions change every frame — the glow appears to shift and breathe.
Higher lines (closer to flames) have higher crack probability and hotter
(brighter) crack colors, maintaining the top-to-bottom heat gradient.

install.py constant:  LINE_CONFIG        (tuple of 5 tuples: base_idx, prob, min, max)
                      TEXT_STYLES        (tuple of 3 pre-built Style objects)
                      TEXT_CRACK_CONFIG  (tuple of 3 tuples: crack_prob, crack_palette_indices)


======================================================================
3. FLICKER PROBABILITY DISTRIBUTION
======================================================================

Two independent rolls per character:

ROLL 1: Does this character flicker at all?

Line-specific intensity — how unstable is this flame zone:

Line   Zone      Flicker Prob   Rationale
-----  --------  ------------   -------------------------
 01    TIPS         100%        Tips are wild, always moving
 02    UPPER         80%        Active combustion zone
 03    BODY          65%        Dense core, some stability
 04    LOWER         45%        Heavy base, slower movement
 05    EMBER         30%        Embers glow steadily, occasional flare

If the roll FAILS, the character stays at its line's base palette index.

ROLL 2: If flickering, how far from base?

Weighted distribution with slight warm bias (flames push energy upward):

Offset   Weight   Probability   Visual effect
------   ------   -----------   --------------------------
  0       40       0.40         Base color (most common)
 +1       18       0.18         One step warmer (brighter)
 -1       17       0.17         One step cooler (darker)
 +2       13       0.13         Two steps warmer (bright flash)
 -2       12       0.12         Two steps cooler (dark dip)

Warm bias: 31% warmer vs 29% cooler.

Rule: Space characters are NEVER colored. Fire doesn't fill empty
space. Only printable characters receive flicker. This preserves the
shape of the flames — the sway motion comes from the whitespace, and
coloring whitespace would destroy that.

install.py constant:  FLICKER_OFFSETS  (tuple of 100 pre-weighted ints)
                      Distribution baked in: (0,)*40 + (1,)*18 + (-1,)*17 + (2,)*13 + (-2,)*12
                      random.choice() on this tuple = O(1), no weight math at runtime.


======================================================================
4. SWAY FRAME DATA
======================================================================

9-frame cycle: 5 forward + 4 reverse (B-slides).
Lines 6-8 are identical in every frame (anchored text body = fuel source).

Frame sequence:  01 -> 02 -> 03 -> 04 -> 05 -> 02-b -> 03-b -> 04-b -> 05-b -> repeat
Frame count:     9
FPS:             8 (125ms per frame)

ABSOLUTE OFFSETS FROM DEFAULT (as implemented in _SWAY_OFFSETS):

These are the ground truth used by the code. Each value is the number
of leading spaces added to (+) or removed from (-) the default line.

Frame              L1    L2    L3    L4    L5
-----------------  ----  ----  ----  ----  ----
01  (default)        0     0     0     0     0
02  (lean left)     -5    -3    -1    -1    +1
03  (deeper left)   -4    -2     0     0     0
04  (pull right)    -1    -1    +1    +2    +1
05  (return)         0     0     0     0     0
02-b (lean right)   +5    +3    +1    +1    -1
03-b (deeper right) +4    +2     0     0     0
04-b (pull left)    +1    +1    -1    -2    -1
05-b (return)        0     0     0     0     0

RESULTING LEADING SPACES (default padding + offset):

Default padding:   26    10     1     1     0

Frame              L1    L2    L3    L4    L5
-----------------  ----  ----  ----  ----  ----
01  (default)      26    10     1     1     0
02  (lean left)    21     7     0     0     1
03  (deeper left)  22     8     1     1     0
04  (pull right)   25     9     2     3     1
05  (return)       26    10     1     1     0
02-b (lean right)  31    13     2     2     0
03-b (deeper right)30    12     1     1     0
04-b (pull left)   27    11     0     0     0
05-b (return)      26    10     1     1     0

Mirror symmetry: Frame N and Frame (N+4) have sign-flipped offsets.
02 [-5,-3,-1,-1,+1] mirrors 02-b [+5,+3,+1,+1,-1].
Frames 01, 05, and 05-b are all identical (default).

Clamp: offsets that would produce negative leading spaces are clamped
to 0. This affects 04-b L3/L4/L5 minimally (1-2 char asymmetry).

install.py constants:  _FLAME_DEFAULTS  (5 raw strings, default flame art)
                       _TEXT_LINES      (3 raw strings, anchored text body)
                       _DEFAULT_PAD     (26, 10, 1, 1, 0)
                       _SWAY_OFFSETS    (9 tuples of 5 absolute offsets)
                       SWAY_FRAMES      (9 pre-computed frames, 8 lines each)


======================================================================
5. IMPLEMENTATION IN install.py
======================================================================

Functions:

  _build_sway_frames()
      Pre-computes all 9 sway frames at module load.
      Each frame = 5 flame lines (default art + offset padding) + 3 anchored text lines.

  _build_fire_frame(frame_idx, rng) -> Text
      Builds one 8-line Rich Text object with per-character fire coloring.
      Flame lines (0-4): iterates each character, rolls flicker, applies FIRE_STYLES.
      Text lines (5-7): applies fixed TEXT_STYLES to full line.
      Spaces are always appended unstyled.

  _make_banner_panel(art) -> Panel
      Wraps a fire frame in the DOUBLE-border banner Panel.
      Title: "peepBeefy", subtitle: version, border: cyan.

  _animate_fire(duration=3.0, fps=8)
      Runs the fire sway animation inside a Rich Live context.
      Creates isolated random.Random() instance (no global state).
      Cycles through SWAY_FRAMES indices at 8 FPS.
      Each tick: fresh flicker roll via _build_fire_frame(), live.update().
      After duration: Live exits, frozen frame persists on screen.

  show_banner()
      Clears screen, calls _animate_fire(), then prints:
      tagline, divider, "INSTALLER  First-time setup", description, divider.


======================================================================
6. PERFORMANCE NOTES
======================================================================

- Pre-built Style objects: 12 fire + 3 text = 15, allocated once at import
- Pre-weighted flicker tuple: random.choice() on 100-element tuple = O(1)
- Text.append() per character: ~300 appends/frame at 8 FPS = 2400/sec (trivial for Rich)
- Live context handles terminal diff updates (only redraws changed characters)
- Total animation: 3 seconds * 8 FPS = 24 frames rendered

ANSI FALLBACK (strict 16-color terminals):

  Line1  bright_yellow   \e[93m
  Line2  yellow          \e[33m
  Line3  bright_red      \e[91m    (reads orange on most terminals)
  Line4  red             \e[31m
  Line5  red dim         \e[2;31m
  Line6  bold white      \e[1;37m
  Line7  white           \e[37m
  Line8  dim             \e[2m

Note: The current implementation uses truecolor hex values via Rich.
The ANSI fallback codes are documented for potential future use in
terminals that don't support 24-bit color.

======================================================================
REVERSE SWAY (B-slides)

Mirror of the forward sway. All absolute offsets are sign-flipped.
Played after forward cycle to create a living, breathing flame.

B-slide art is NOT hand-crafted — it is computed at module load by
applying mirrored offsets to the default flame lines. The code handles
this in _build_sway_frames().
